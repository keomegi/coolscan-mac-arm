Activity

Saved settings

Keeps the settings for one kind of work under a name. A set holds how you scan and save (film, resolution, dust removal, grain, focus and exposure, colour space, file names), not the folder, the roll or notifications. Saving under a name that exists replaces it. "Not saved" means the settings in use match no saved set.

Film

The colour of a negative is worked out from each frame on its own when it is scanned. Every frame's film is also measured and kept with the roll (FilmColour.json in its folder), by itself. Nothing is corrected from it while scanning: to correct a roll's colours from its film, open the roll in Scanned pictures and see "How to use film correction" there. It can be put on one picture or the whole roll and taken off again.

When it focuses (never on the thumbnail pass). Autofocus each frame (default): on a frame's first preview or scan; later passes of that frame reuse it until a new strip, Eject, Reset or a new focus point. Autofocus on every pass: before each pass. Same focus for all frames: the last Autofocus or Calibrate. Manual: never. A cropped scan is focused on the whole frame. The Activity list notes each time it really focuses.

Roll

One folder per roll; each strip adds to it, numbering on. Pick a roll in the list to carry on in it. The description is saved as Description.txt in the roll folder. Changing the roll number renames the folder and the R… part of its file names; nothing is deleted.

Scan quality

No dust removal on ordinary B&W film: its silver blocks infrared just like dust does. Chromogenic B&W film (C-41, such as Ilford XP2 or Kodak BW400CN) is made of dyes and can be cleaned: choose it as the film type.

Dust removal is switched off when you choose Kodachrome: its cyan dye blocks infrared much as dust does, so the cleaner can paint over parts of the picture. You can tick it again to try it; check the result closely.

Dust removal finds dust and scratches in the infrared picture. IR Clean is the app's own cleaner and changes only the dirty spots; nkscan is the cleaner of the scanner driver (nkscan), the older method, and changes more of the picture (and needs a second pass in Archive mode). Multi-sample: cleaner shadows, that many times slower. Multi-Exposure: a second, longer pass for the dense parts of the film; helps slides most and at least doubles the time. Grain reduction and Restore faded colours are the app's versions of Nikon's GEM and ROC, off unless you pick a level.

Save as

Archive mode is switched at the top of the window.

Archive mode (switch at the top) saves two folders from one pass: RAW (the untouched scan, infrared as a 4th channel) and Positive (the finished picture). It always scans the whole frame. It is on when the app starts; to change that, see Scanning in this window. Scan only the cropped part: a cropped frame scans faster because only the crop is read; unticked, the whole frame is scanned and cut when saved. All files are 16-bit TIFF. Make a folder for each roll: on, each roll gets its own "Roll 005 - film" folder inside the folder, and names end in the roll number (_R005); off, the pictures go straight into the folder, with no roll folder and no roll number in the name, and the Roll card is hidden.

File names

A name is: prefix, scan date, picture number, roll number (only with a folder for each roll). The number counts on from the pictures already there; type one to start from it once. A name in use is skipped, never overwritten, unless you tick "Allow overwriting": then a typed number is used as it is, and Scan first shows which pictures would be replaced and waits for you to confirm. "Photo taken" sets the date photo apps sort by; with only a year, today's day and time in that year are used.

Start a new roll?

The next scans go into a new folder and numbering starts again at 1. Film details are optional: they name the folder and go into the files. The camera goes into the files only.

Replace pictures that are already there?

    The old files are replaced and cannot be brought back. "Use free numbers" scans with the next numbers nobody uses instead.

    Scan again with the same settings?

    Nothing was changed since that scan (settings, crop, frame position), so you would get the same picture a second time.

    Archive mode

    On: each frame is saved twice from one pass of the scanner.

    • RAW: the scan exactly as it came from the scanner, with its infrared picture. Nothing is done to it, and it is always the whole frame.
    • Positive: the finished picture, with dust removal, colour and your crop.

    Because the RAW file is kept, you can make the finished picture again later with other settings on the Re-develop page, without the film or the scanner.

    Off: only the finished picture is saved. It takes about half the disk space, but a picture can then only be made again by scanning the film again.

    The app starts with Archive mode on. To change that: Settings, "Start in Archive mode".

    Settings

    Save as

    What is saved from each scan, and where.

    File names

    How the saved pictures are named and numbered.

    Thumbnail

    One fast, low-resolution pass over the whole strip. It finds the frames and gives each its small picture. Nothing is saved.

    Ticked: the thumbnail pass runs by itself when a strip goes in. Unticked: press Thumbnail when you want it.

    Preview

    A quick scan of one frame, sharper than the thumbnail, to judge focus, colour and crop before the real scan. Nothing is saved.

    Preview every frame when film goes in (off to begin with): after the thumbnail, each frame gets a preview scan by itself, which takes a while longer per frame; it needs "Thumbnail when film goes in" ticked. Preview scan dpi: the resolution of a preview. Once a frame is scanned, the big picture shows the scan itself.

    Unticked makes a preview quicker: autofocus takes about 7 to 18 seconds a frame on your LS-40, metering a few more. Without autofocus the lens stays where it was, so the preview can look soft; the scan still focuses the frame as set on the left. Without metering the preview reuses the exposure of the last preview, so a much darker or lighter frame can look wrong. A frame previewed this way gets a red AF off or AE off mark, like in Nikon Scan. The scan itself is not changed by these.

    While a strip is scanned

    What the app does while it works through the frames of a strip.

    If a frame fails, the rest of the strip is still scanned unless you tick "Stop at the first frame that fails". The scan log is ScanLog.txt in the roll folder.

    When a strip is done

    What happens after the last frame of a strip is saved.

    Ticked: the scanner pushes the film out when the strip is finished, ready for the next one. Unticked: the film stays in until you press Eject.

    Frame positions

    Where each frame sits on the strip. For when pictures sit off to one side, or a frame shows the start of the next picture.

    For when pictures sit off to one side in their frames, or a frame shows the start of the next picture. Ticked: "Strip offset ◀ ▶" appears above the big picture and moves every frame on the strip together (Nikon Scan called this Strip Film Offset), and small arrows above the preview move one frame alone. The frame pictures redraw as you press; scans read the moved frames.

    Ticked: after the thumbnail, the app looks for the gaps between the pictures and moves the frames onto them by itself, for example when the strip was cut through its first picture. Frames with no clear gap stay where the scanner put them. Unticked: frames stay where the scanner put them, as in Nikon Scan.

    Archive mode

    Archive mode saves two files of each frame from one pass: the untouched scan (RAW) and the finished picture (Positive). It is switched at the top of the main window.

    Ticked: the app always starts with Archive mode on. Unticked: it starts the way you left the switch at the top.

    Auto crop

    Cuts the film edges, the holder and the gap between pictures off a frame.

    Crop each frame automatically: every frame is cropped as it is shown; unticked, a frame is cropped only when you press Crop. You can always move the box by hand. Cuts further in by: takes that much more off each side it cuts, for a clean edge. Cuts: all four sides, or only the two ends along the strip.

    Learning from your crops

    Auto crop can learn to crop the way you do. It learns on this Mac only: your pictures, the small copies kept to learn from and what is learned from them stay on this Mac and are never sent anywhere.

    Ticked: every crop you draw by hand, here or in Scanned pictures, is kept to learn from, and it learns again by itself after every 10 new crops. What it learned is used only where it lands closer to your crops than the model that came with the app and the built-in rule. Unticked: none of your crops is kept and nothing more is learned; Auto crop goes on using what it has.

    To learn, it keeps a small greyscale copy (200 pixels) of each frame you crop, on this Mac only (never on GitHub). Ticked: they are deleted each time it has learned. What it learned stays, and crops made in Scanned pictures are read again from there next time, but crops from the scanning screen can then no longer be learned from again. Off by default: keeping them lets it learn better as you add crops.

    Reset to default forgets what was learned from your crops and goes back to the crop model that came with the app from GitHub. The learning pictures stay until you delete them, so with learning ticked it learns from them again; untick it to stay with the default.

    Auto rotate

    Works out which way up each picture goes, and turns it or suggests a turn.

    Ticked: the app works out which way up each picture goes, with a ready-made model trained on a great many photographs (80 MB, fetched once from GitHub, run on the GPU). What to expect: the scanned picture is very likely turned the right way by itself: the scan is looked at once more just before its files are saved, and on the pictures of the first eleven rolls that was right for every one. A preview scan is nearly as good. A thumbnail is not: it has too few pixels (about 6 in 100 come out wrong), so on thumbnails it only suggests a turn under the picture and never turns by itself. So frames can look the wrong way up after the thumbnail pass and still be saved the right way up. A turn it made by itself is marked ↻ Auto rotated on the frame card and not asked about; when one is wrong, turn it back with the turn buttons and it learns from that. A frame you turned or cropped by hand is never turned again. Your own turns are still kept to learn from, and are what it falls back on without the ready-made model (no MLX, or the file not fetched yet). Unticked: nothing is kept, learned, turned or suggested.

    Auto rotate first finds the frame direction of the whole strip: when the frames come out upside down (normal for some films), every frame is turned 180° by itself, and a purple bar over the frames asks whether the frames look correct (Accept or Reject). Your answer is kept for the roll: later strips of it are not asked about unless one comes out the other way round. Tick this to also have the switch in the Film card, to set it by hand.

    Learning from your turns

    Auto rotate can also learn from the turns you make by hand. It learns on this Mac only: your pictures, the small copies kept to learn from and what is learned from them stay on this Mac and are never sent anywhere.

    Ticked: each turn you make by hand, here or in Scanned pictures, is kept to learn from, and it learns again by itself after every 10 new turns. Unticked: none of your turns is kept and nothing more is learned; Auto rotate goes on turning and suggesting with what it has.

    Reset to default forgets what was learned from your turns and goes back to the turn model that came with the app from GitHub. The ready-made model is not touched. The learning pictures stay until you delete them (Crop tab), so with learning ticked it learns from them again; untick it to stay with the default.

    When a strip is done

    Tells you when a strip is finished, so you can walk away while it scans.

    Not installed. Notifications come from an optional file, notify.py, that is kept out of git. To use them, put it in the coolscan_app/extras folder of the app (Claude can put it there for you), then start the app again.

    Pick one or more ways, then press Send a test.

    How to set each one up

    • Banner on this Mac: tick it and press Send a test. If no banner shows, open System Settings › Notifications and allow notifications for Script Editor (macOS shows the app's banners under that name).
    • Run a shortcut: in the Shortcuts app, make a shortcut that receives text and does something with it, for example Send Message to your phone or Show Notification. Type its name here; the list suggests your shortcuts.
    • ntfy: install the free ntfy app on your phone and subscribe to a topic with a name nobody would guess. Type the same name here. The notification goes through the public ntfy.sh server, so it leaves out your film notes.
    Logs and cache

    The app's own record of what it did, for finding out what went wrong.

    The app writes down everything it does, every error, and what the scanner reports, in the logs folder next to the app (one file a day, in JSON). Older files are deleted. 0 keeps them all.
